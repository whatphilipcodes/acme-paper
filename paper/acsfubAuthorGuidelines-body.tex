\begin{abstract}
    Continual Learning can be computationally demanding, making on-device deployment challenging. In an effort to perform dimensionality reduction that can be dynamically adjusted based on user feedback and running on-device, this paper proposes a dataset generation pipeline, providing the foundation for such a model. With a subset of the Wikipedia dataset as a source, text embeddings are generated using pretrained models such as all-MiniLM-L6-v2, all-mpnet-base-v2 and nomic-embed-text-v1.5 \cite{nussbaumNomicEmbedTraining2025}. These high-dimensional vectors are then reduced to two-dimensional coordinates by applying UMAP and t-SNE. The resulting datasets are compared and analyzed regarding their visual legibility and spatial-semantic coherence. Alongside this paper, the generation pipeline and resulting datasets are published.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The contemporary information landscape is both vast and inflationary. This is particularly evident in technology-related fields, where professionals must constantly update, learn, and organize information to maintain effectiveness. An increasingly large portion of this cognitive effort is being supplemented by generative Machine Learning (ML) models: In the 2024 Stack Overflow developer survey 63.2\% of respondents reported reliance on tools that are implementing Artificial Intelligence (AI) \cite{AI2024Stack}.

However, the centralized architecture through which these systems are typically deployed, diminishes user ownership and autonomy. This concern extends beyond mere convenience, as recent research also suggests cognitive implications: A study by Kosmyna et al. indicates that the use of ChatGPT for essay writing is associated with decreased neural connectivity and recall ability \cite{kosmynaYourBrainChatGPT2025}.

Instead of relying on full knowledge synthesis by large language models, a more balanced approach lies in the usage of AI for automation of the organizational overhead around cognitive tasks. One particularly important aspect is the management and accessibility of human generated information. Cartographic visualizations resemble an excellent example for


As an example, spatial mnemonic devices like the memory palace can be a powerful tool to strengthen ones retention ability \cite{brownMakeItStick2014}.

Spatial mapping is particularly suitable for this purpose, as it mirrors how humans intuitively organize and navigate information. Representing a text corpus as a coordinate system provides an interpretable structure where semantic proximity translates into spatial closeness.

This focused scope of application for AI drastically reduces the required resources for training and inference, therefore bringing full on-device processing and local Continual Learning (CL) within reach. To lay the groundwork for the training of models that combine the spatial output with light computational requirements, this paper proposes a dataset generation pipeline based on established text embedding and Dimensionality Reduction (DR) algorithms. It addresses the following practical question: How can sBERT embeddings combined with Dimensionality Reduction techniques like PCA, t-SNE, and UMAP be used to generate a training dataset that maps encyclopedic texts to spatial coordinates?

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:rel-work}

Embedding generation is the prerequisite for understanding and comparing textual data. Sentence-BERT (sBERT) \cite{reimersSentenceBERTSentenceEmbeddings2019a} produces contextual embeddings that account for word order and surrounding semantics, making it a well-established foundation in many downstream natural language processing (NLP) tasks. In this work, sBERT serves as the primary framework to generate high-dimensional embeddings. However, these embeddings alone are not directly interpretable, requiring an additional DR step for visualization.

Dimensionality reduction techniques such as PCA, t-SNE, and UMAP are widely applied to high-dimensional data for visualization and interactive exploration \cite{smilkovEmbeddingProjectorInteractive2016}. PCA provides a stable linear reduction and is frequently used as a preprocessing step. t-SNE remains a popular choice for uncovering fine-grained local clusters but is computationally demanding. OpenTSNE implementations improve efficiency and scalability, making it more suitable for large datasets \cite{policarOpenTSNEModularPython2024}. UMAP, in contrast, emphasizes global structure while still preserving local neighborhoods \cite{mcinnesUMAPUniformManifold2020a}. This property makes UMAP complementary to t-SNE: While t-SNE excels at separating dense clusters, UMAP offers continuity across regions of semantic space.

Finally, commercial systems such as Nomic Atlas \cite{duderstadtAtlasWhitepaperScalable} adopt a similar approach, embedding large-scale corpora and projecting them into low-dimensional interactive maps. Atlas is designed for scalability and integrates with projection frameworks such as NOMAD \cite{nussbaumNomicEmbedTraining2025}. However, parts of the system remain proprietary, limiting reproducibility. In contrast, the dataset generation pipeline proposed here is aimed at small models suitable for continual learning while remaining fully open-source.

\subsection{Pipeline Implementation}
\label{sec:pipeline}

This pipeline implements a comprehensive text embedding and dimensionality reduction workflow for processing textual datasets using multiple embedding models. The pipeline consists of three primary stages: Text embedding generation, preprocessing with principal component analysis, and two-dimensional projection using both t-SNE and UMAP algorithms.

As a prerequisite, this paper assumes an existing source dataset of texts. Due to scope, size and availability the wikipedia dataset (20231101.en subset) was chosen \cite{WikimediaWikipediaDatasets2025}.

The proposed pipeline was developed and tested locally on a desktop workstation with the following specifications:
\begin{itemize}
    \item An Intel Core i9-10980XE (3.00GHz) CPU
    \item an NVIDIA Quadro RTX 5000 (16GB) GPU
    \item 128 GB RAM.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Embedding Generation Stage}

The pipeline employs three distinct pre-trained sentence transformer models for text embedding generation:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item all-MiniLM-L6-v2: A lightweight model based on the MiniLM architecture, optimized for efficiency while maintaining competitive performance on semantic similarity tasks.
    \item all-mpnet-base-v2: A model utilizing the MPNet (Masked and Permuted Pre-training) architecture, which combines the advantages of BERT and XLNet through bidirectional attention and permutation-based training.
    \item nomic-embed-text-v1.5: A specialized embedding model with extended context length capabilities, specifically designed for clustering applications and requiring task-specific prompting.
\end{enumerate}

For the basic models (all-MiniLM-L6-v2 and all-mpnet-base-v2), text inputs are processed without additional prompting. However, due to their limited context windows, texts exceeding the maximum token length are truncated during tokenization, potentially resulting in information loss for longer documents.
The nomic-embed-text-v1.5 model is configured with a clustering-specific prompt ("clustering: ") to optimize embedding quality for downstream clustering tasks. Its larger context size enables processing of complete articles without truncation, preserving the full semantic content of input texts.
Batch processing is implemented with model-specific batch sizes: 32 samples for basic models and 16 samples for nomic-embed-text-v1.5, balancing computational efficiency with memory constraints.

\subsubsection{Preprocessing Stage}

Prior to dimensionality reduction, embeddings undergo a two-step preprocessing procedure:

All embedding vectors are L2-normalized to unit length, transforming the similarity metric from cosine similarity to Euclidean distance. This normalization ensures that subsequent distance-based algorithms operate in a geometrically consistent space.

A PCA transformation reduces embeddings to 50 dimensions. This intermediate dimensionality reduction serves dual purposes: Noise reduction and computational efficiency enhancement for subsequent non-linear dimensionality reduction algorithms. While not strictly necessary for UMAP unifrom preprocessing makes the results more comparable.

\subsubsection{Dimensionality Reduction Stage}

The pipeline implements two complementary non-linear dimensionality reduction techniques for 2D visualization: UMAP (Uniform Manifold Approximation and Projection) and t-SNE (t-Distributed Stochastic Neighbor Embedding). Final 2D coordinates from both UMAP and t-SNE are normalized to the range [-1, 1] using Min-Max scaling to ensure consistent coordinate spaces across different reduction techniques and facilitate comparative analysis.

The synthetic dataset was successfully generated and subjected to dimensionality reduction analysis using the two prominent visualization techniques UMAP and t-SNE.

Considering \autoref{fig:umap-comparison} and \autoref{fig:tsne-comparison} there are distinct characteristics revealed in both visualization approaches: UMAP exhibited very pronounced global structures with a landscape-like appearance, effectively preserving the overall topology and global relationships within the data. In contrast, t-SNE demonstrated superior space utilization, distributing data points more evenly across the available visualization area while maintaining local neighborhood structures. These complementary strengths suggest that the choice between methods depends on whether preserving global structure or maximizing space efficiency is the primary analytical objective.

The selection of dimensionality reduction technique ultimately emerges as a matter of analytical preference, balancing space utilization against global recognizability. This choice reflects the inherent trade-off between maximizing the efficient use of visualization space and maintaining clear global structural patterns that facilitate pattern recognition and interpretation. The decision framework requires consideration of the specific analytical goals and the relative importance of local versus global data relationships.

A critical factor in the embedding evaluation process was the context length limitation of the embedding models. Notably, the Nomic embedding model was the only approach capable of capturing full articles in their entirety, while all other embedding methods required truncation of the input text. This limitation significantly impacts the quality and completeness of the semantic representations, as truncated articles may lose crucial contextual information and semantic relationships that extend beyond the model's context window, potentially affecting downstream analysis and visualization quality.

\subsection{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The performance and effectiveness of embedding models demonstrate significant sensitivity to data domain specificity, highlighting the importance of model selection based on the characteristics of the target dataset. Domain-specific training and fine-tuning can substantially influence how well embedding models capture the nuanced semantic relationships and terminology unique to particular fields or text types. This domain dependency suggests that embedding model performance cannot be universally generalized across different types of content, requiring careful consideration of the alignment between model training data and the specific domain of application to achieve optimal representation quality.

\section{Conclusion / Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Future work should consider the exploration of additional dimensionality reduction approaches beyond the traditional UMAP and t-SNE methods examined in this study. Alternative techniques such as PaCMAP \cite{JMLR:v22:20-1061} offer promising avenues for investigation, potentially providing different trade-offs between computational efficiency and visualization quality that could complement the existing methodological landscape.

A central challenge for continual learning on-device lies in computational overhead. Since both embedding generation and dimensionality reduction are required, two resource-intensive algorithms would have to be executed repeatedly, restricting feasibility in constrained environments. One approach to overcome this, could be the training of a model that directly outputs two dimensional embeddings for text inputs. The generated datasets could serve as the first step towards implementing such a solution.

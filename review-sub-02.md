### `Overall Review`

The paper presents a novel investigation into the influence of Stanislavski's acting principles on LLM prompting. By applying performance theory to AI interaction, the author explores a creative method for potentially increasing the emotional believability of chatbot responses. This research is a good fit for Creative Technologies, as it successfully bridges the gap between the cultural practice of performance and the technical field of machine learning, reflecting the interdisciplinary orientation of our study program.

Despite its innovative premise, the paper has several weaknesses: The prominent term "subtextual intelligence" is not thoroughly discussed or given a clear definition. Furthermore, while Section 1.2 claims the proposal of a framework, the experiment is not explicitly abstracted as a generalizable approach. Lastly, a significant ambiguity remains regarding whether chain-of-thought reasoning was utilized. If it was, the author should have stated the models' operational modes and included reasoning outputs in the appendix. If not, the conclusions drawn about the method's effectiveness are weakened since the additional context might have yielded similar improvements.

The paper's primary strengths lie in its experimental component and the clarity of its research question. The author makes a strong and extensive effort to relate Stanislavski's system to the challenges of creating emotionally resonant LLM interactions, providing a solid theoretical foundation for their practical experiment. This strong relation between theory and application makes the paper's core argument coherent and comprehensible.


### `Overall Rating: 4/5`

The paper is commended for its creative approach in applying Stanislavski's acting principles to LLM prompting and its solid theoretical introduction and consolidation of both. However, the rating is lowered by critical weaknesses: A key term ("subtextual intelligence") is left largely unclear, an advertised framework is not clearly articulated and the ambiguity about the use of chain-of-thought reasoning partly undermines the validity of the conclusions.
